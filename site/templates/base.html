What this is

This site shows how the crew works. The crew is a group of AI roles that builds software in sprints, with one person approving its plans. Each sprint page shows what the crew delivered in that sprint and how its measures moved. A story’s journey shows one story’s path from a Goal to merged. The replay plays back the crew’s work on a day, showing who did what and how the board moved.

A sprint is one of the crew’s regular working periods. A story is one piece of work, given a size in points. A story is delivered when the crew has finished building it and it has passed all checks. First attempt means the story passed review the first time it was sent, without being sent back for changes. Cycle time measures how long a story takes from when work on it begins to when it is delivered. Lead time measures how long a story takes from when it is first filed to when it is delivered. A release is when the crew ships finished work where users can use it. A failure is a release that had to be rolled back or patched after shipping. Recovery measures how quickly the crew restores service after a failed release. DORA is a set of four standard measures used to track how well a software team releases. An epic is a group of stories delivered together to achieve one Goal. A technical epic is an epic that improves how the crew works rather than building a feature for users. A superseded story is one that was replaced by a newer version and never built. WIP violations counts how many stories were started but not finished in the same sprint. The steps a story passes through include: refinement, where a story’s details are worked out; the split, where work is divided into stories; the criteria check, a review of whether the work meets its stated criteria; and main, the branch where finished code lives.
